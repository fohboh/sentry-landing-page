It isn't a report. It's what makes the money collectable.

The money was already yours. It left in fees that were never in your contract — and it stays gone because proving that costs more than most operators can spend.

Every certification cycle ends in a CAAR: a Certified Audit & Action Report. Not a dashboard you have to interpret. Not a finding you have to defend in a meeting. A sealed, self-authenticating document that names the amount you're owed, the rule that proves it, and the contract clause it violates — line by line, with the arithmetic shown.

That's the difference between suspecting you're being overcharged and having something you can file.

Every dollar traced to a rule. One of 198 deterministic rules fired, and the report names which one and what condition triggered it.
Sealed the moment it's generated. SHA-256 against the final document, written to an append-only Vault. Nobody can quietly revise it — including us.
Built to survive the pushback. Structured against FRE 902(11) and 803(6) so the vendor argues about the money, not about your spreadsheet.
